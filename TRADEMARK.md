**Owner:** Jesse Sunil, original creator of DeluluLang, holds all rights in the "DeluluLang" name
and any DeluluLang logos or wordmarks (together, the "Marks").

**Why this exists, and what it does *not* do.** The code of DeluluLang is licensed under
Apache-2.0 (see `LICENSE`): you may use, modify, sell, and distribute it freely, for any purpose,
by anyone — human, company, government, AI system, or robot. A software license governs *code*; it
does not govern a *name*. This policy governs the name, and only the name. Its single purpose is
that no one can be misled about **what is the original DeluluLang and who created it**. It follows
the same well-established separation that Rust, Python, and Mozilla use: permissive code license +
a trademark policy for the name.

This policy is modeled on established open-source trademark practice. It is intended to be read and
followed in good faith, not to be a trap. When in doubt, ask.

## 1. What you may always do

You do **not** need permission for any of these:

- **Use DeluluLang.** Write programs in it, run it, build it from source, deploy it, teach it.
- **Sell software written in DeluluLang, and sell services around it.** The Apache-2.0 license
  already grants this; nothing here limits it.
- **Redistribute DeluluLang unmodified** under the name "DeluluLang" — for example, packaging the
  official release for a distro or a mirror — provided it is genuinely the unmodified work and you
  keep the `LICENSE` and `NOTICE` files intact.
- **Refer to DeluluLang truthfully.** Say your software is "written in DeluluLang," "built with
  DeluluLang," "compatible with DeluluLang," or "a DeluluLang library/tool." This is nominative
  use and it is always allowed.
- **Discuss, review, critique, and teach** DeluluLang, including using the name in articles, talks,
  courses, and book titles that are about DeluluLang.

## 2. What requires a different name

If you distribute a **modified version of the language itself** — a fork, a derivative language, a
reimplementation, or a distribution whose compiler/runtime behavior differs from the official
release — you must **give it a different name** and must not present it as the original DeluluLang.
Specifically:

- **Do not name a derivative "DeluluLang"** or use a name confusingly similar to it as the name of
  your product. Choose your own name. You may still state, truthfully, that it is "based on
  DeluluLang" or "a DeluluLang derivative."
- **Do not claim, or imply, that your version is the official or the original DeluluLang**, or that
  it is endorsed by or affiliated with the DeluluLang project or Jesse Sunil, unless that is
  actually true and agreed in writing.
- **Do not remove or alter the authorship record.** The `NOTICE` file names Jesse Sunil as the
  original creator; that attribution rides with every distribution under Apache-2.0 §4, and it may
  not be changed. You may add your own name as the author of *your* derivative and its changes —
  never as the author of the original language.

The principle in one line: **use the name to point at DeluluLang, never to relabel something else
as DeluluLang.**

## 3. Logos

Do not modify a DeluluLang logo and present the result as a DeluluLang logo, and do not use a
DeluluLang logo as the logo of your own product in a way that implies it is official. Referential
use (e.g. a "works with DeluluLang" badge) is fine.

## 4. Selling

You may sell software written in DeluluLang, sell a build of a *renamed* derivative, and charge for
support, hosting, or services. You may **not** sell something *as* "DeluluLang" (or "the original
DeluluLang," or "DeluluLang Pro/Enterprise/…") if it is a modified version — that is the naming
rule in §2, applied to a sale.

For anything this policy does not clearly cover, or to request permission for a use it does not
allow, contact the owner. Good-faith requests are welcome. This policy may be updated; updates
apply going forward and never retroactively revoke the Apache-2.0 rights in the code, which are
irrevocable by the terms of that license.
